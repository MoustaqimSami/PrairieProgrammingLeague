# Prizes

The season has a **$1,000 prize pool** across all 6 contests, given out as ten $100 prizes. Prizes are awarded by **draw**: every ticket you earn is one more entry. All draws happen after the final contest in April, on a livestream.

## Earning tickets

| How | Tickets |
| --- | --- |
| Compete in an in-person contest | **10** per student |
| Solve a problem in an in-person contest | **6** per problem, for **each** person on your team |
| Upsolve a problem you didn't solve in person | **2** per problem |

- To earn in-person tickets you must be physically at the university.
- Every person on the team gets the full 6 tickets for each problem the team solves, so a bigger team means more tickets for everyone. A team of 3 that solves a problem earns 18 tickets between them.
- Upsolving is individual, and only counts problems you didn't solve during the in-person contest.

**Example:** Sam competes in person on a team of 3. The team solves 4 problems, so Sam earns 10 + (4 × 6) = 34 tickets, and so does each of Sam's teammates. During the week Sam upsolves 2 more problems for 4 more tickets, for 38 total that month.

## The draws

There are **10 draws of $100**, held in this order:

1. **City draws:** one per city that hosts at least 5 of the in-person contests and has at least 3 students in the draw. Eligible cities are Edmonton, Calgary, Lethbridge and Saskatoon.
2. **First-year draw:** first-year undergraduate students only.
3. **Second-year draw:** second-year undergraduate students only.
4. **General draws:** everyone, as many as it takes to reach 10 draws in total.

## The fine print

- Each student can win only once. Once you've won a draw, you're out of the later ones.
- There's no universal definition of what year you're in. Each university's coach has the final say on each student's year.
- The organizing committee may adjust for unforeseen events or misconduct, including removing some or all of a student's tickets. Their decisions are final.
